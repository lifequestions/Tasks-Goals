The six suits

Sorted by the decision, not the psychology.

Textbooks group these by mental cause, which is useless at the moment of choosing. These are grouped by the room you are standing in.

In use

Three ways people run it.

Before a decision

Pull the suit that matches — hiring, money, speed — and read the nine or ten cards in it. Five minutes, and the obvious traps are named before anyone has argued for anything.

In the room

Deal one card face-up to each person and let them argue that side. It gives the quiet objection a licence to be said out loud, which is most of what a pre-mortem is for.

After it goes wrong

Spread the suit over the decision you regret and find the card that describes what actually happened. That is the one to pin above the desk.

Questions

Is this the same content as the site?

Yes — the same 74 entries, the same counter-moves. The site is for browsing and searching; the deck is for the twenty seconds before you decide something, away from a screen.

Why 74 and not 188?

Because a deck you can actually read in one sitting beats a list you never finish. These are the ones that change decisions rather than trivia questions.

Do I need the poster too?

No. The poster is the whole table at a glance for a wall; the deck is the detail in your hands. They are the same thing at two distances.
