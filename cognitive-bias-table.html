How it is arranged

Six families, one per kind of decision — money, power, people, self, information, speed. Each family owns a block of columns rather than a single one, and the column heights inside it are what cut the staircase. Before a decision of one kind, read that block.

The dot

Each element carries a second mark: the mental shortcut underneath it — too much information, not enough meaning, the need to act fast, or the limits of memory. Different columns, same four causes, which is why one counter-move often fixes several.

Bonds

Hover an element to see the biases it reinforces. Few act alone: anchoring feeds framing, sunk cost leans on loss aversion, and the blind spot covers all of them.

Counter-moves

Every card ends with one. Knowing a bias by name does not dissolve it — the fixes that work are procedural: written predictions, fixed criteria, outside views, and someone else holding the pen.
