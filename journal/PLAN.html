Undercurrent — a journal that notices

A private journal for your iPhone that you write in or talk to. It keeps track of the people, places and themes in your life, draws them as a map, and tells you the connections you haven't spotted — every week, month, quarter and year.

01 · The idea

Four steps, over and over

Everything in the app is one of these four things. The name is what it looks for: the current underneath the surface of what you write.

CaptureWrite, or tap the mic and talk. A daily question if you're stuck. Or note a quick insight: “I always feel flat after seeing Jordan.”
ReadEach entry is read for the people, places, themes and activities in it, and for how you felt about each one there.
ConnectEvery mention joins the map. Plain arithmetic spots patterns; Claude spots the ones arithmetic can't.
ReflectA written reflection for each week, month, quarter, year and the whole journal, with open questions to sit with.

03 · How it finds connections

Two readers and some arithmetic

The part that matters most, finding the connections, works even with no internet and no AI. Claude adds depth on top.

The phone reads first, instantly. Apple's language tools pick out people's names and places, a starter list of themes (work, sleep, money, anxiety…) and activities (running, drinking, scrolling…), and score how each sentence feels. It's free and private, and it's enough to draw the map.

Then Claude reads it again, if you've added a key. It finds themes the starter list doesn't know, like “self-doubt” or “Dad's health”. It knows “Sam” and “Samantha” are the same person, judges how you felt about each person in that entry, and occasionally links it to something from weeks ago: “This is the third time a visit home has been followed by a heavy Monday.”

Arithmetic runs after every entry. It compares how entries feel when something is in them with how they feel when it isn't. That's your friend example exactly: if entries mentioning Jordan read heavier than the rest, it says so, with no AI involved. It also spots things that nearly always turn up together, days of the week that run heavy, and people who've quietly disappeared from the page.

Your own insights get checked

When you note something like “I think I keep saying yes to Jordan out of guilt”, it's saved as your insight. Every reflection is given your insights and asked whether the entries bear them out. So the app tests your theories as well as offering its own.

05 · Knowing yourself

The more you answer, the better it reads you

Every questionnaire result goes to Claude with each reading and reflection. It's the difference between “you seemed low” and “you tend towards introversion, so three social evenings in a row may be the cause”. Only freely usable, research-backed questionnaires are included.

06 · Privacy and care

It's your inner life, so it stays yours

Everything lives on your iPhone. Entries, the map and the patterns are stored in the app's own database. Dictation happens on the phone where the model supports it.

Claude is optional and switchable. Your API key goes in the iPhone Keychain. “Keep everything on this iPhone” turns every call off, and the app still works. By default, Anthropic doesn't train on API data.

You can take it all with you. Export produces one JSON file of every entry, mention, insight and reflection.

It never diagnoses. The instructions tell Claude to observe, not judge, and to say which things are clear and which are only a hunch.

Still to add before anyone else uses it

Face ID lock on open. A gentle signpost to real help if entries show signs of crisis. And a small server that holds the API key, because a key inside an app is fine for one person but not for many.

07 · Roadmap

Four phases

1

Your own journal In this repo

A working SwiftUI app for iOS 17 and later, installed on your phone from Xcode. Write and dictate; phone and Claude reading; the map; patterns; the five kinds of reflection; asking your journal; two questionnaires; reminders; export; a sample journal to try it with.

2

Living with it

Use it daily for a month, then tune what it notices. Add the things that make it a habit.

  • Face ID lock, widgets, Action Button
  • Apple Health: sleep, activity, HRV
  • On this day; “what follows”; the time-lapse map
  • iCloud sync between iPhone and iPad (the database needs small changes first)
3

Deeper self-knowledge

  • Attachment, values and the full Big Five
  • Life chapters, and a beautiful year in review
  • Photos and voice kept with entries
4

If you ever want to share it

A small server to hold the key, sign in with Apple, a privacy policy, crisis signposting, TestFlight for friends, then the App Store.

08 · Getting it on your iPhone

About twenty minutes, once

  1. Install Xcode 16 or newer on a Mac, from the App Store.
  2. Open journal/Undercurrent.xcodeproj from this repository.
  3. Sign it. Click the Undercurrent project, then the target, then Signing & Capabilities, and choose your Apple ID as the Team. A free Apple ID works, but the app has to be reinstalled every seven days. The $99/year developer account removes that and gives you TestFlight.
  4. Plug in your iPhone, pick it at the top of Xcode and press Run. The first time, your iPhone will ask you to trust the developer in Settings → General → VPN & Device Management.
  5. Try it with made-up data. In the app, go to You → Settings → Load a sample journal. The map fills in, and the pattern finder should spot the made-up Jordan straight away. Erase it when you're ready to start for real.
  6. Add Claude. Create an API key at console.anthropic.com, paste it into Settings and tap Save and test.

09 · Cost

A few dollars a month

With Claude Opus 5, reading one entry costs about 4–6 cents, depending on length and how much context goes with it. Writing every day comes to roughly $2–4 a month, reflections included. Claude Sonnet 5 costs about 40% as much and is a setting away. Without a key it costs nothing.

10 · For you to decide

Questions worth answering before phase 2

  1. The name. Undercurrent is a working title. Others: Threadline, Tributary, Kin, Between.
  2. iPhone only, or iPad and Mac too? iCloud sync is the main work either way.
  3. How much should it say unprompted? Right now it notices after entries and in reflections. Some people want a nudge (“You haven't mentioned Maya in a month”); others find that intrusive.
  4. Apple Health? It's probably the richest extra signal, especially with an Oura ring, but it's more data to trust it with.
  5. Ever for other people? If so, phase 4 matters earlier than it looks.
