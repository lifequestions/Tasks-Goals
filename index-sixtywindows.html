The questions don't come naturally

Sitting down to ask your father about his childhood feels strange, so it gets postponed. Having the questions written out already removes the awkward part.

Photographs don't talk

Most families have thousands of pictures and not one recording of how he actually sounds when he's telling a story he's told a hundred times.

It's never the right moment

Until suddenly it's the wrong one. The people who do this always say the same thing afterwards: they wish they had started ten years earlier.

The things people worry about

Answered plainly

Who can see it?

Only people you send the link to. Pages are never listed, never searchable, and there is no directory anyone can browse. The videos are unlisted, so they don't appear on any channel or in any search.

He hates being filmed.

Most of them do, for about four minutes. Sit off to the side so he's talking to you rather than a lens, and don't tell him it's an interview. If he really won't have a camera on him, record just his voice over a photograph — it works nearly as well.

What if he dies before we finish?

The page becomes a memorial with one change of setting: his dates close, the best answers move to the top, and the questions he never got to stay on the page rather than being quietly deleted. You are not left with a half-finished to-do list.

What happens to it if you go out of business?

You get every file. That promise is written down, not implied — and you can download the whole archive yourself at any time, video and transcripts, without asking us.

Will you use this to make an AI version of him?

No. Not now, not later, not as an upgrade. His recordings are not training data, and we won't make a chatbot that pretends to be your father.

Ask him this weekend.

One question, forty minutes, a phone propped on a stack of books. That's the whole of it — and it's the part nobody regrets.
