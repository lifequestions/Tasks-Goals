Step one

We send you the question

One a week, or all sixty at once if you're in a hurry. Every question comes with the follow-ups that turn a thirty-second answer into a proper story — the ones you'd never think to ask in the moment.

Family can add their own too, credited on the page: asked by Tom, aged 9.

Step two

He answers it on a phone

Prop it on a stack of books, sit beside it rather than behind it, ask him, then stay quiet. No app, no editing, no camera crew — and no need for him to be any good at technology, because he never touches it.

Camera-shy? Record his voice over a photograph instead. It works nearly as well.

Step three

It becomes his page

Upload, and the page builds itself: his photograph, his dates, his answers, each one a window you press to hear him. One private link for the whole family — his sister, your kids, the cousins in Australia.

Nothing is public. No search engine, no directory, no strangers.

And at the end of it

A book, in his own words

Every answer is transcribed automatically, so his stories exist as words as well as video. Choose the ones you want, add the photographs, and we print a hardback — the thing that gets passed round at Christmas and ends up on a shelf for forty years.

From £59. One copy or fifteen.

Straight answers

The things people worry about

Who can actually see it?

Only the people you send the link to. Pages are never listed or searchable, there's no directory to browse, and the videos are unlisted so they appear on no channel and in no search results.

He hates being filmed.

Most of them do, for about four minutes. Sit off to the side so he's talking to you and not a lens, and don't call it an interview. If he really won't have it, record his voice over a photograph.

What if he dies before we finish?

The page becomes a memorial with one change of setting. His dates close, his best answers move to the top, and the questions he never got to stay on the page instead of being quietly deleted.

What if you go out of business?

You get every file, and that's written down rather than implied. You can download the whole archive — video and transcripts — at any time, without asking us.

Will you make an AI version of him?

No. Not now, not later, not as an upgrade. His recordings are not training data and we won't build a chatbot that pretends to be your father.

Is it too late if he's already gone?

Not necessarily. Families use the same pages the other way round — everyone else recording their memories of one person. Same questions, different chairs.

Ask him this weekend.

One question, forty minutes, a phone propped on a stack of books. It's the part nobody ever regrets.
